Waivers: FAAB

This league uses FAAB (Free Agent Acquisition Budget) instead of a rolling waiver-priority order. Every team starts the season with the same virtual budget. When a player hits the waiver wire, each interested team submits a blind bid — a secret dollar amount from their remaining budget — for that player. Nobody sees what anyone else bid. When the waiver period processes, the highest bidder wins the player and that amount is permanently deducted from their budget; everyone else keeps their bid in reserve. If two teams submit the same top bid, ESPN's waiver tiebreaker order decides it. There's no refill during the season, so spend deliberately — blowing your budget on a Week 3 flyer means nothing left for the Week 12 injury-replacement scramble.

Injured Reserve (IR)

The IR slot is a bonus roster spot for players who are genuinely banged up. Only players ESPN has flagged with an IR, IL, or Out designation are eligible to occupy it. Stashing a player there doesn't cost you a bench spot — it opens one up, so you can use the extra room to add a free agent or waiver pickup while the injured player sits out. To activate someone off IR, you move them back into an open bench or starting spot, dropping a player first if your roster is full. Players who are merely suspended don't qualify — IR is for injuries, not for suspensions.

Section 4

Playoff Format: The Losers Bracket

Missing the playoffs doesn't mean your season is over — it means you're playing for something worse than a trophy.

Regular Season

All 12 teams play a full regular season. Final standings set seeding for both brackets.

→
Top Seeds

The top — teams enter the Winners Bracket and play for the championship.

→
Bottom Seeds

The remaining — teams enter the Losers Bracket, seeded by regular-season finish.

The Losers Bracket runs on the same weekly schedule as the Winners Bracket — it isn't optional and it isn't over once you're eliminated from title contention. Every team in the bracket keeps setting a real lineup every week; ESPN re-seeds each round based on who wins and loses within the bracket, so a hot week can still move you up the ladder. After the final round, the bracket resolves into a full ranking from best-of-the-worst down to dead last. That last spot is the one that matters most: whoever finishes at the bottom of the Losers Bracket — meaning last place in the entire league — is the one who faces the punishment.
